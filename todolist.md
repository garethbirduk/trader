# Todo List

- **Route the remaining decision sites through the judgement engine — priority.** Several call sites still gate on raw `appraisalAccuracy[category]` scalars or have no appraisal at all, when the judgement-engine doc lists them as Condition/Price consumers. These are the cheapest wins for landing the engine's design intent across the simulation, and they're the prerequisite for NPC trading specialists to actually *feel* different — per-actor expertise/j only matters if every decision routes through the engine. In rough order of cost-to-payoff:
  - **`world/actor-planner.ts:198-201` — "interesting-lot count" on the docket.** Currently tests `profile.appraisalAccuracy.get(item.category) >= interestThreshold` per known lot. The planner's whole point is to express per-actor preferences over today's docket; today the test is the actor's *generic* skill scalar, not their belief about the *specific lot*. Swap for `estimatePriceBand` on the lot and gate on perceived value vs floor (or vs a threshold). One-line conceptual change, but high leverage — planner routing is the most-fired decision in the game.
  - **`world/detail-unlock.ts:172-175` — "is this locked headline interesting?".** Same raw `appraisalAccuracy >= threshold` test on the subject item kind. Same swap. Player-facing because it gates the autonomy-driven unlock spending NPCs do in pub chat.
  - **`world/clearance-autonomy.ts` — NPC booking has no appraisal.** Eligible NPCs (dealer/fence tag + cash + at-phone-venue) roll a flat `bookChancePerHour` regardless of whether the listing is junk or treasure. The judgement-engine doc explicitly lists clearance booking as a **Condition + Price** consumer. Today's behaviour means a clearance specialist with high furniture-arm expertise has no edge over a generalist on undervalued listings — kills the "Boyce is the furniture-clearance king" archetype the NPC-specialists item depends on. Wire `estimateLotValue` (or `estimatePriceBand` if condition is unknowable from a listing headline) into the booking decision.
  - **`world/pool-claim-autonomy.ts` — pool claim has no appraisal.** Gated only on `attemptChance`, `affordableQty`, and the pool's published unit price. Doc lists pool claims as a **Price-only** consumer. An expert should hesitate on an overpriced pool; today everyone takes whatever's on offer. `estimatePriceBand` against the pool's unit price, with the claim probability scaled by how much of a deal the actor thinks it is.
  - **Auction seller reserve (conditional).** The auction-house floor in `regional-clearance.ts:75` is structural and correctly bypasses the engine — there's no character behind the price. If named characters ever become auction sellers (probate-style off-loaders, or the player consigning lots), their reserve should come from `estimatePriceBand` rather than a fixed `floorFractionOfRetail`. Park until that feature shows up.
  - *(Bribe willingness in `market-stall-autonomy.ts` / `bribe.ts` is the character-arm play — reading the patrol officer. Correctly deferred behind the heat-layer prerequisite below; not a miss.)*

- **More NPC trading specialists — differentiate the cast along channel + role axes.** Today's cast is shapely on *identity* (info-traders, shopkeepers, off-map whales) but generic on *trading style*: the four dealer-NPCs (Boyce, Denzil, Monkey-Harris, Trigger) all run market stalls, all do pubdeals, all claim from pools. Real-world dealers carve out lanes — and the planner already has the slots to express those preferences but doesn't differentiate strongly enough. Axes to lean on:
  - **Channel preference.** Some only do pubdeals (negotiators); some only run market stalls (volume-pushers, often adhoc-trading risk-takers); some live at the auction (Sotheby's regulars); some only chase clearances (estate scavengers). Today every dealer does all of them. Differentiate via per-actor planner weights on `pub` / `market` / `auction` / `shop` — already in `EconomicsConfig.planner.baseWeights` but applied uniformly. Per-actor overrides give characters distinct shapes.
  - **Wholesaler vs retail.** Some dealers prefer trading to *other dealers* (wholesale margin, large lots, no end-customer headache). Others sell direct to the public at markets/shops. Wholesalers' pub-deal autonomy might prefer trading-actor buyers; retail-leaners prefer being at the market in front of footfall. Could surface as a per-actor `trade_orientation: "wholesale" | "retail"` tag, planner-aware.
  - **Information role without trading.** An old man like Albert sits at the Legion drinking, picks up gossip across the pub / shop / cafe routine, never trades. Pure information accumulator. His value to the player is the chat — he's the cheapest detail-unlock target on the headlines feed (under two-tier gossip). Today he's tagged civilian; the design intent is fuller. Maybe add a `info-collector` role flag and tune chat-yield knobs per-actor.
  - **Clearance specialists.** Someone whose planner heavily weights the newspaper for clearance listings and who reliably out-books generalists. Could be an existing actor (Boyce as the furniture-clearance king?) or a new face.
  - **Category expertise.** Bidder profiles encode per-category appraisal accuracy and the judgement engine now drives valuation off it. Lean into that harder: some NPCs have *one* speciality category they're sharp on and are clueless about everything else. Today many actors are competent generalists; sharper character profiles would create more "Mickey doesn't know furniture, exploit it" situations. Per-arm j (`actor_arm_j`) is another knob — a "decisive but wrong" actor reads differently from a "hesitant but right" one even at the same expertise.
  - **Risk appetite.** Legit-stall vs adhoc-trading; forward-sell aggressive vs forward-sell never; willingness to chase clearances at high fees. Per-actor `risk_appetite` axis the planner consults.
  - **Social score.** The character arm (docs/judgement.md) is now wired; per-actor `socialScore` is seeded by the skin. Tune individual values to make pubdeal asymmetries land — currently Driscolls 0.90, Trigger 0.20, the rest spread in between. The buyer's flaw-detection bonus = α × (buyer_social − seller_social) means a low-social punter eats scams a high-social punter spots.
  - **Implementation shape.** Each axis is small in isolation — extend the placeholder skin's actor specs with new optional fields, thread them through the planner's per-actor candidate scoring, and expose them on the actor profile so the player can read each NPC's character at a glance. The data is already there for most of this (bidder profiles, planner weights, role tags, social scores); the work is in giving every NPC distinct *values* on these axes rather than the same baseline.

- **Heat / intimidation layer.** Today there's no systemic mechanic for personal danger or police pressure beyond Slater's bribe-loop and the existing bust-drama. Concept: a `heat` resource carried by each actor (and the player) that accumulates from risky activity — handling fenced goods, refusing intimidation, being witnessed at a bust. High heat raises bust probability, gates entry to certain locations or deals, and changes how other actors treat you (clean dealers refuse to buy from a hot seller; Driscolls might *avoid* a high-heat target who'd attract police attention rather than pile on). Decays over time if you keep your nose clean.
  - Heat is partly observable to other actors via the rumour mill — interacts with the two-tier gossip layer above. "Heard Del's been hot lately" is itself a piece of detail-tier info.
  - Unblocks the Driscoll intimidate-pubdeal mechanic (preceding item): refusal lands a heat hit, accepting carries hot stock you then have to launder.
  - Slater's bust-roll math is the existing consumer — wire heat in as one of the inputs alongside witness-seeded drama.
  - Touches: actor state (`heat` field with decay), bust resolution, pub-deal variants, possibly diary alerts when your heat crosses thresholds, and any "will trade with you" gates on autonomy.

- **Character-arm noise v2 — route social reads through the judgement engine.** v1 (`socialScore` on actors) is a public scalar with an exact `(buyer_social − seller_social)` delta. v2 makes each actor's *perception of another's character* a band, mirroring how condition v2 noised the truth read.
  - **Per-actor `character_expertise` and `character_j`.** New knobs on the actor profile; defaults set so most civilians stay near v1 behaviour. Trigger gets low character_j (mis-reads a wrong'un as a friend); the Driscolls get high character_j to match their high socialScore (they read marks well in both directions).
  - **`estimateCharacter(perceiver, target, rng)` returns a noisy perceived social score.** Distinct from v1's source of dumbness: in v1 Trigger eats scams because his own `socialScore` is low; in v2 he *also* eats them because his read of others is noisy. The two compound.
  - **Fully asymmetric delta.** Each side uses their own perceived value of the other: `α × (buyer_perceived_seller_social − seller_perceived_buyer_social)`. Doubles perception calls per pub-deal but produces the "both sides think they have the upper hand" moment, which is exactly the drama the arm exists to create.
  - **Seed advances on meaningful events, not per tick.** A read is stable through routine encounters and re-draws on events the perceiver would notice — a deal that went badly with the target, a pubdeal with stakes, a witnessed bust, a heat-status change. Avoids shimmer on re-render and gives the "I used to trust him, then…" coherence that constant re-rolls would destroy. Implies a per-(perceiver, target) memo keyed off a list of invalidating events.
  - **UI shows the perceiver's perception, not truth, when the player is observing NPC↔NPC interactions.** Watching Trigger walk into a Driscoll deal, the player sees *Trigger's* read of the Driscolls (which is wrong). The player's own POV on others still renders truth (or the player's own perception, on the same rules as condition v2). Parallels how perceiver-j-gated reads work for goods.
  - Worth measuring how often the social delta actually fires in a typical session before committing — if pub-deal frequency is low the cost-to-payoff ratio drops and a cheaper variant (e.g. only the flaw-detection path goes asymmetric) becomes worth reconsidering.

- **Note for any future re-inspect mechanic.** Condition reads stay as fresh RNG draws on each call — no per-(actor, lot) memoisation. A re-inspect mechanic, if ever added, is designed around the actor genuinely seeing a different sample on the second look (and the player learning to weight repeated reads), not around remembering a first impression.
