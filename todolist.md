# Todo List

- **Implement the judgement model** — see [docs/judgement.md](docs/judgement.md). A unified framework replacing today's piecemeal perception knobs (`appraisalAccuracy`, `flawTypeDetection`, no on-the-fly character read) with a single composable model: every belief is a band, centre comes from expertise, width comes from judgement-j, samples are mixture-shaped. Four parallel arms (Identity / Condition / Price / Character) compose multiplicatively into final valuations. UI gets a 10-stop blue→red palette with per-perceiver j-band collapse — playing Trigger literally lowers the resolution of the screen, which is where player-skill compensates for character-skill. Substantial: schema expansion (~28 dials per actor), new per-category generic-anchor table, migration of `appraiseLot` + all pub-deal/market/shop call sites, plus the UI colour refactor. Doc has the full architectural brief and a sketched implementation order.

- **More NPC trading specialists — differentiate the cast along channel + role axes.** Today's cast is shapely on *identity* (info-traders, shopkeepers, off-map whales) but generic on *trading style*: the four dealer-NPCs (Boyce, Denzil, Monkey-Harris, Trigger) all run market stalls, all do pubdeals, all claim from pools. Real-world dealers carve out lanes — and the planner already has the slots to express those preferences but doesn't differentiate strongly enough. Axes to lean on:
  - **Channel preference.** Some only do pubdeals (negotiators); some only run market stalls (volume-pushers, often adhoc-trading risk-takers); some live at the auction (Sotheby's regulars); some only chase clearances (estate scavengers). Today every dealer does all of them. Differentiate via per-actor planner weights on `pub` / `market` / `auction` / `shop` — already in `EconomicsConfig.planner.baseWeights` but applied uniformly. Per-actor overrides give characters distinct shapes.
  - **Wholesaler vs retail.** Some dealers prefer trading to *other dealers* (wholesale margin, large lots, no end-customer headache). Others sell direct to the public at markets/shops. Wholesalers' pub-deal autonomy might prefer trading-actor buyers; retail-leaners prefer being at the market in front of footfall. Could surface as a per-actor `trade_orientation: "wholesale" | "retail"` tag, planner-aware.
  - **Information role without trading.** An old man like Albert sits at the Legion drinking, picks up gossip across the pub / shop / cafe routine, never trades. Pure information accumulator. His value to the player is the chat — he's the cheapest detail-unlock target on the headlines feed (under two-tier gossip). Today he's tagged civilian; the design intent is fuller. Maybe add a `info-collector` role flag and tune chat-yield knobs per-actor.
  - **Clearance specialists.** Someone whose planner heavily weights the newspaper for clearance listings and who reliably out-books generalists. Could be an existing actor (Boyce as the furniture-clearance king?) or a new face.
  - **Category expertise.** Bidder profiles already encode per-category appraisal accuracy. Lean into that harder: some NPCs have *one* speciality category they're sharp on and are clueless about everything else. Today many actors are competent generalists; sharper character profiles would create more "Mickey doesn't know furniture, exploit it" situations (see the notebook idea above).
  - **Risk appetite.** Legit-stall vs adhoc-trading; forward-sell aggressive vs forward-sell never; willingness to chase clearances at high fees. Per-actor `risk_appetite` axis the planner consults.
  - **Implementation shape.** Each axis is small in isolation — extend the placeholder skin's actor specs with new optional fields, thread them through the planner's per-actor candidate scoring, and expose them on the actor profile so the player can read each NPC's character at a glance. The data is already there for most of this (bidder profiles, planner weights, role tags); the work is in giving every NPC distinct *values* on these axes rather than the same baseline.

- **Stage 9 — sweep infrastructure** (from the design doc): thin headless-runner wrapper that takes a config matrix × N seeds, runs each cell, dumps a CSV of per-run metrics (wealth distribution, clearance rates, deal volume, bankruptcy count, heat events). Lets you hammer combinations and tune knobs against objective signal.

- **Player takeover — any NPC, not just Del.** Today the player slot is hardcoded to Del Boy (the `player` actor code). Generalise so the player can take over *any* actor at session start — Boyce, Trigger, Mickey-Pearce, even a shopkeeper. The actor's planner/autonomy hands the wheel to the player; everything else (schedule, lockup, bidder profile, role) keeps working unchanged. Implies separating "is-player" from the actor's identity — `player` becomes a runtime status (which actor the human is driving), not a role tag. Touches: session setup (actor selection UI), autonomy bypass (skip planner picks for the player-driven actor), and any code that hardcodes `code === "player"` or the Del-specific player actor id.

- **Driscoll Brothers — intimidate-pubdeal mechanic.** Today the Driscolls are villain-only (no trading, no bidder profile). Concept: they corner dealers in the pub and *force* a buy of stolen goods at unfavourable prices — pub-deal autonomy but where one side has a heat threat instead of a price argument. Refusal carries a heat / safety cost; accepting moves dodgy stock onto your inventory at a markup. Needs: a new pub-deal variant (or a flag on existing pub-deals) carrying the threat; a stolen-goods pool the Driscolls hold; resolution that lands the heat hit if you refuse. Don't bake this in until the broader heat/intimidation layer exists.

- **Heat / intimidation layer.** Today there's no systemic mechanic for personal danger or police pressure beyond Slater's bribe-loop and the existing bust-drama. Concept: a `heat` resource carried by each actor (and the player) that accumulates from risky activity — handling fenced goods, refusing intimidation, being witnessed at a bust. High heat raises bust probability, gates entry to certain locations or deals, and changes how other actors treat you (clean dealers refuse to buy from a hot seller; Driscolls might *avoid* a high-heat target who'd attract police attention rather than pile on). Decays over time if you keep your nose clean.
  - Heat is partly observable to other actors via the rumour mill — interacts with the two-tier gossip layer above. "Heard Del's been hot lately" is itself a piece of detail-tier info.
  - Unblocks the Driscoll intimidate-pubdeal mechanic (preceding item): refusal lands a heat hit, accepting carries hot stock you then have to launder.
  - Slater's bust-roll math is the existing consumer — wire heat in as one of the inputs alongside witness-seeded drama.
  - Touches: actor state (`heat` field with decay), bust resolution, pub-deal variants, possibly diary alerts when your heat crosses thresholds, and any "will trade with you" gates on autonomy.
